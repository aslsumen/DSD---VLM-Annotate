# Detection Engine Evaluation Results

English | [简体中文](EVALUATION.zh-CN.md)

Updated on 2026-11-22. Results are interpreted against the KPIs defined in [REQUIREMENTS §6](../../docs/REQUIREMENTS.md#6-kpis-proposed-to-confirm-with-the-customer).

In the latest long-video evaluation, VideoJev detected 258 of 300 cooking events and 112 of 238 driving events. While the point estimate for cooking recall reached the 85% target, driving recall and the complete clip rates across both domains remain below target thresholds. In a smaller controlled comparison sharing the same pipeline, swapping the detection backend to Gemini Decision caused overall recall to drop from 57.3% to 33.3%. As a result, VideoJev is retained as the primary detection backend, and automated in-product verification remains disabled. Rationale for frame sampling parameters and score thresholds is detailed in the ablation studies below.

## 1 New KPI Evaluation

### 1.1 Metric Definitions

**Ground truth (GT)** events are human-annotated actions with explicit start and end timestamps. **Candidate events** (or proposals) are temporal intervals predicted by the engine, and **exported clips** are the video segments generated by padding margins around candidate intervals. Candidate intervals and exported clips serve distinct evaluation purposes: the former determines whether an event was successfully located, while the latter evaluates whether the exported clip is sufficiently complete for human review.

Temporal Intersection over Union (**IoU**) is defined as: duration of candidate ∩ GT ÷ duration of candidate ∪ GT. For a given video and prompt, candidates and GT annotations are matched greedily one-to-one in descending order of IoU; an event is considered detected (found) if IoU ≥ 0.3. Each candidate and each GT event can participate in at most one valid match.

| Metric | Definition and Denominator | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Event Recall | Number of detected GTs ÷ Total GT count (missed events are included in the denominator) | ≥ 85% |
| Complete Clip Rate (CCR) | Proportion of matched candidates whose exported clip covers the GT span, allowing up to 2 seconds missing at either boundary | ≥ 85% |
| Acceptance Rate | Number of matched candidates ÷ Total candidate count (including candidates generated by absent prompts) | Reported (target TBD) |
| Mean Boundary Adjustment | Sum of absolute errors between candidate start/end points and GT boundaries ÷ (2 × number of matches), in seconds | Reported (target TBD) |
| Candidates per Present Prompt | Total candidates generated by present prompts ÷ Number of present prompts | Measures review workload |
| Candidates per Absent Prompt | Candidates generated by prompts confirmed absent by ground truth annotations; reported as both total count and per-prompt average | Measures spurious review workload |

Here, ground truth annotations simulate a human reviewer: a successful match is treated as an accepted proposal with its boundaries adjusted to the GT span. These figures serve as **automated proxy metrics for review workload**, not empirical measurements of end-user acceptance rates or human editing time. Crucially, CCR and boundary adjustments are computed only over successfully detected events and do not capture false negatives; an excessively wide candidate interval may achieve a high CCR while incurring substantial boundary adjustment penalties.

"Absent" status is determined based on dataset annotations and prompt filtering rules. For cooking, negative prompts were drawn from unrelated recipes and filtered for lexical non-overlap; they have not undergone exhaustive manual inspection to guarantee complete absence from the raw video footage. Consequently, these figures estimate reviewer burden rather than serving as definitive false-positive ground truth. Strict-overlap metrics (such as R@IoU 0.5/0.7, R1, and mIoU) are diagnostic diagnostics and are not formal acceptance targets in this report.

### 1.2 Datasets and Experimental Setup

Evaluations were conducted between 2026-11-20 and 2026-11-22. Each raw long video was processed individually without concatenation; all 53/53 videos completed successfully with zero missing outputs. Hyperparameters were held fixed throughout this evaluation and were not tuned on these 53 videos.

| Dataset | Videos & Total Duration | Ground Truth (GT) | Prompts |
|---|---|---|---|
| YouCook2 (Cooking, validation split) | 33 videos, 3.65 hours, 29 recipe categories | 300 action steps | 300 present, 99 absent; 12.1 prompts/video on average |
| ROAD (Driving, Oxford RobotCar) | 20 videos, 2.75 hours | 238 ego-vehicle action events | 7 fixed prompts/video; 83 present, 57 absent in total |

Ground truth for ROAD was extracted from 12 fps ego-vehicle action annotations, where adjacent intervals sharing the same label with a gap under 1 second were merged. The "stopped" label in ROAD denotes an idling ego-vehicle, which differs from descriptions like "slows down or stops for a red light" used in the BDD-X comparison.

The test environment was an RTX 3070 Laptop (8 GB VRAM), Ryzen 7 5800H CPU, and ~15 GB RAM available to WSL, with partial model weights offloaded to CPU. The model evaluated was VideoJev-4B at revision `b614f1a6248ba1f169cd3bec9e4392758b566e5d`. Input videos were standardised to 854×480 at 30 fps. The configuration was based on `configs/rtx3070.yaml` with **Gemini verification explicitly disabled**: coarse scan with a 30-second window, 20-second stride, and 1 fps / max 32 frames; refinement at 2 fps / max 64 frames; fusion parameters `high=0.25`, `low=0.15`, and `min_event_score=0.5`.

### 1.3 Results and Findings

Bracketed ranges represent 95% bootstrap confidence intervals computed via 2,000 video-level resamples with seed 0. Recall is aggregated over total ground truth counts, and CCR is aggregated over total matched candidate counts, rather than taking unweighted macro-averages of per-video percentages. Overall acceptance rate is calculated across all generated candidates.

| Metric | YouCook2 (Cooking) | ROAD (Driving) |
|---|---|---|
| Event Recall | **86.0% [81.1%, 90.3%]** (258/300) | **47.1% [39.5%, 56.1%]** (112/238) |
| Complete Clip Rate (CCR) | **77.5% [70.3%, 84.7%]** (200/258) | **82.1% [72.5%, 91.5%]** (92/112) |
| Overall Acceptance Rate (incl. absent prompts) | 12.3% (258/2,100) | 9.0% (112/1,242) |
| Mean Boundary Adjustment | 4.2 s [3.5, 5.0] | 7.9 s [5.6, 11.1] |
| Candidates per Present Prompt | 5.85 (1,756 total) | 9.20 (764 total) |
| Candidates per Absent Prompt | 3.47 (344 total) | 8.39 (478 total) |

ROAD recall broken down by action category:

| Action | Detected / GT | Recall |
|---|---|---|
| Turn right | 42/48 | 87.5% |
| Turn left | 32/54 | 59.3% |
| Stop | 35/100 | 35.0% |
| Overtake | 3/12 | 25.0% |
| Change lane left | 0/12 | 0.0% |
| Change lane right | 0/12 | 0.0% |

**Conclusions:**
- **Cooking:** While the point estimate for cooking recall exceeds 85%, the lower bound of the confidence interval is 81.1%, indicating that consistent attainment of the target is not yet statistically assured (this CI illustrates sampling uncertainty rather than imposing a new formal gate). Cooking CCR remains below the 85% target, and detected proposals frequently require boundary trimming.
- **Driving:** The primary failure mode in driving is false negatives (missed detections), particularly for stopping, overtaking, and lateral maneuvers (lane changes were entirely missed). The 82.1% CCR applies solely to the 112 successfully detected events and does not offset the 126 missed events.
- **Review Burden:** Both domains exhibit low acceptance rates (~9–12%), with absent prompts still generating numerous spurious candidates (3.5 to 8.4 candidates per negative prompt), creating non-trivial manual review overhead.
- **Domain Generalization:** These public dataset benchmarks highlight domain discrepancies and cannot substitute for validation on actual client footage.

### Speed KPIs

**The unit of measurement is a single "one video × one prompt" search task.** Metrics report the actual elapsed processing seconds for that individual task along with its single-prompt task Real-Time Factor (RTF = processing seconds ÷ video duration). Each search executes as an independent backend job; multi-prompt batch totals or per-prompt averages from multi-query runs must not be used as product speed KPIs.

Measurements were retaken on 2026-11-22 on the same RTX 3070 Laptop (8 GB VRAM) using the production API and worker. The codebase corresponded to main baseline `a1b8279` with visual feature caching changes from `02cdaf7`; Gemini verification was disabled. The table below compares search times for the identical video and prompt B with and without video feature caching; the model was pre-loaded in both conditions.

| Video | Video Duration | Prompt B (No Video Cache) | Prompt B (Reusing Video Cache) | Processing Time Reduction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| YouCook2 (Cooking) `-AwyG1JcMp8` | 307.467 s | **178 s (RTF 0.58)** | **97 s (RTF 0.32)** | 45.5% |
| ROAD (Driving) `2014-06-25-16-45-34_stereo_centre_02` | 394.5 s | **303 s (RTF 0.77)** | **186 s (RTF 0.47)** | 38.6% |

Cooking prompt B was `place the dumplings above a pot of boiling water`; driving prompt B was `the ego vehicle turns left`. Cache reuse leveraged visual features extracted and persisted during an earlier search for prompt A on the same video. Because prompt B may introduce new refinement positions, not all refinement windows hit the cache. The empty-cache control was measured by deleting the video, verifying cache eviction, re-uploading the file, and querying prompt B again with the model already warm. Predicted candidate boundaries and scores were identical with and without caching (2 candidates for cooking, 10 for driving).

The initial search for prompt A was also measured as a single-prompt job in each domain:

| Video | Prompt A | Initial Model State | Video Feature Cache | Processing Time | Single-Prompt Job RTF |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| YouCook2 (Cooking) | `place the filling on a wrapper and press the edges together` | Unloaded (cold start, includes load time) | Empty | 231 s | 0.75 |
| ROAD (Driving) | `the ego vehicle is stopped` | Warm (reused in same worker) | Empty | 322 s | 0.82 |

**Measurement Boundaries:** Elapsed time is calculated as `job.finished_at − job.started_at` recorded by the backend. It includes model loading (if applicable), inference, backend preview extraction, and result persistence; it excludes network upload time, pre-job queuing before the worker claims the task, and post-upload staging. It represents production end-to-end task turnaround for a single prompt, not bare engine inference RTF or total upload-to-candidates latency. All six jobs completed successfully. Each condition represents a single observation with one-second timestamp granularity; no median, p90, or service-level performance guarantees are implied.

**Conclusions:** On these ~5–6.6 minute test videos with a warm model and empty cache, a single prompt took 178 s and 303 s; reusing existing visual features reduced this to 97 s and 186 s. Cache benefits must be calculated strictly between matched runs of prompt B; time differences between dissimilar prompts A and B are invalid comparisons. Caching results reflect the evaluated feature-cache branch. Full upload-to-proposal latency, performance on longer (10–30 minute) videos, and speed under the Gemini backend require separate empirical measurement.

Raw logs, prompt texts, and cache/model states are archived in [single_prompt_timing.json](../benchmarks/results/2026-11-22/single_prompt_timing.json); benchmarking protocols are documented in [PERFORMANCE.md](PERFORMANCE.md). Multi-prompt batch runtimes across the 53 long videos were used exclusively for accuracy evaluation and are excluded from product speed reporting.

## 2 Same-Pipeline Comparison with Gemini Decision API

### 2.1 Setup and Test Data

This benchmark compares the **detection backends**: VideoJev-4B versus `gemini-decision-1`. Both backends were integrated into the identical engine pipeline—sharing coarse scanning, temporal fusion, proposal refinement, and clip export logic—with identical window sizes, frame sampling rates, and thresholds, and without post-hoc verification. Gemini does not emit raw timestamps directly; instead, it processes sampled frames within a sliding window and answers for discrete sub-intervals whether "the specified action occurred between second $a$ and second $b$". The resulting probabilities form a temporal score curve fed into the downstream fusion engine. A 30-second coarse window contains 15 discrete time bins, while refinement windows use denser binning.

This evaluates how the two backends and their respective score adaptations perform under an **identical, fixed configuration**. Because their underlying probability distributions and prediction mechanisms differ, and thresholds were not independently tuned for Gemini, these findings reflect comparative behavior under this specific pipeline rather than an absolute upper bound on Gemini's capabilities across arbitrary configurations.

This comparative run was conducted on 2026-11-19 across three earlier composite benchmark videos; **Gemini was not evaluated on the 53 long videos discussed in Section 1**:

| Benchmark Set | Source Composition | Duration | Present / Absent Prompts | Ground Truth (GT) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BDD-X | Concatenation of 20 dashcam clips | 802.3 s | 7 / 1 | 72 |
| YouCook2 a | Concatenation of 3 recipe videos | 1,816.7 s | 12 / 6 | 12 |
| YouCook2 b | Concatenation of 3 distinct recipe videos | 1,526.0 s | 12 / 6 | 12 |

All prompts searched the full composite video. Each cooking composite contains only 12 GT events (where a single event shifts recall by 8.3 percentage points); this setup was designed to observe backend discrepancies on identical footage rather than serve as a replacement for long-video KPI validation.

### 2.2 Results and Findings

Acceptance rate was recalculated using the unified methodology of this report, where the denominator includes candidates generated by absent prompts. Note that `event_precision` in legacy evaluation JSON files accounted only for present prompts and must not be conflated with the overall acceptance rate reported here.

| Benchmark Set | Detection Backend | Event Recall | Complete Clip Rate (CCR) | Overall Acceptance Rate | Mean Boundary Adjustment | Candidates / Present Prompt | Total Absent-Prompt Candidates |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BDD-X | VideoJev | 47.2% (34/72) | 94.1% | 22.2% (34/153) | 3.7 s | 21.29 | 4 |
| BDD-X | Gemini Decision | 18.1% (13/72) | 100% (13/13) | 15.5% (13/84) | 4.5 s | 12.00 | 0 |
| YouCook2 a | VideoJev | 100% (12/12) | 83.3% | 3.9% (12/308) | 7.8 s | 20.75 | 59 |
| YouCook2 a | Gemini Decision | 83.3% (10/12) | 90.0% | 14.5% (10/69) | 5.5 s | 5.75 | 0 |
| YouCook2 b | VideoJev | 75.0% (9/12) | 77.8% | 3.2% (9/277) | 4.3 s | 17.17 | 71 |
| YouCook2 b | Gemini Decision | 75.0% (9/12) | 88.9% | 10.2% (9/88) | 6.6 s | 7.25 | 1 |

Across all 96 ground-truth events, VideoJev detected **55 events (57.3%)**, whereas Gemini detected **32 events (33.3%)**. Total proposals dropped by 67.3% (from 738 to 241), but at the cost of substantial false negatives. On BDD-X, detections for "slows down or stops for a red light" dropped from 16/22 to 2/22; Gemini's 100% CCR applies exclusively to the 13 detected events and should not be interpreted as superior overall grounding.

**Conclusions:** VideoJev is retained under this pipeline configuration. While Gemini significantly curtails false alarms on negative prompts, driving recall degrades drastically, and cooking results show no consistent advantage. Qualitative inspection revealed instances where driving states were merged into overly broad temporal spans, pointing to residual issues in bin-level prompt adaptation and temporal calibration. Additionally, 5 sliding windows in YouCook2 a were blocked by Gemini API content safety filters; these unhandled windows were retained in the results without retry. API rate limiting and quota contention also introduce unpredictable latency variance, precluding any claims of throughput advantage.

## 3 Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments isolate the impact of individual architectural components or parameter sets against a controlled baseline to justify design decisions. We explicitly distinguish between full end-to-end reruns and offline re-fusion runs over archived score curves. Unmeasured metrics are left empty rather than imputed. Processing times in this section serve solely for relative computational cost comparisons under identical query batches; production single-prompt latency is documented in Section 1.4.

### 3.1 Sampling Density and Event Retention Threshold

These paired experiments were evaluated on the Charades-STA test split. Because Charades-STA was part of VideoJev's training corpus, these results serve as in-domain engineering heuristics rather than out-of-domain acceptance evidence. All quality metrics were measured prior to verification.

| Experiment & Sample Size | Baseline → Variation | Recall | Complete Clip Rate (CCR) | Zero-Candidate Query Rate | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frame Sampling Density (56 videos, 174 queries) | Coarse 0.5 fps / 16 frames, Refine 1 fps / 32 frames → Coarse 1 fps / 32 frames, Refine 2 fps / 64 frames | 59.8% → 67.2% | 84.6% → 76.9% | 21.3% → 10.9% | Increases recall (+7.4 pp) at the expense of clip completeness (-7.7 pp); adopted as a deliberate trade-off |
| `min_event_score` (300 videos, 822 queries, full rerun) | 0.9 → 0.5 | 69.0% → 77.1% | 77.8% → 78.6% | 12.3% → 1.1% | Retain 0.5 provisionally; pending tuning on independent held-out data |

`min_event_score` establishes the minimum peak confidence a proposal must reach along the raw score curve; it is distinct from Gemini verification probability thresholds. The zero-candidate query rate measures queries yielding zero proposals; it is not equivalent to the overall miss rate, as queries with proposed intervals that do not match the GT in time still constitute misses.

Doubling sampling density increased average processing time (coarse scan + refinement) from 14.81 s to 21.35 s per video (+44%). Lowering `min_event_score` from 0.9 to 0.5 reduced candidate acceptance rate from 76.8% to 73.7%. Because every Charades-STA query has a corresponding GT event, this split cannot quantify the false-positive penalty on negative (absent) prompts. Given the volume of negative proposals observed in long videos, 0.5 remains a provisional setting.

### 3.2 Denser Refinement and Narrower Fusion

| Experiment & Sample Size | Baseline → Variation | Recall | CCR / Trade-off | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Refinement Density (52 videos with short events, 175 queries, full rerun) | 2 fps / 64 frames → 4 fps / 120 frames | 70.9% → 70.3% | CCR 87.1% → 85.4%; Refinement time per video 12.20 s → 19.84 s | Rejected: no quality gain observed |
| Single-Threshold Fusion (300 videos, 822 queries, offline score re-fusion) | `high=0.25, low=0.15` → `high=low=0.5` (both `min_event_score=0.5`) | 77.1% → 65.9% | Acceptance rate 73.8% → 63.0%; CCR unrecorded | Rejected: severe recall penalty |
| Peak-Relative Trimming (same archived scores) | Standard fusion → Retain only interval spanning ≥ 50% of peak score (both `min_event_score=0.5`) | 77.1% → 69.1% | Acceptance rate 73.8% → 63.7%; CCR unrecorded | Rejected: inferior recall and precision |

The 175 queries in the refinement density study cover all annotations across the selected 52 videos, rather than isolating events under 5 seconds. To prevent out-of-memory (OOM) errors at 4 fps, the GPU weight budget was reduced from 6 GiB to 5 GiB and the CPU offload budget increased from 9 GiB to 10 GiB; the latency increase reflects both additional sampling frames and expanded CPU offloading, not frame rate alone.

The fusion ablations were performed offline without reinvoking the model. Because initial raw predictions originated from runs using a 0.9 threshold, newly uncovered proposals from lowering the threshold to 0.5 did not uniformly have refinement data; thus, these re-fusion results are constrained by archived coverage. Proposed narrowing schemes must be compared against the 77.1% baseline (which also uses `min_event_score=0.5`), rather than the 69.0% figure from the 0.9 run. Findings indicate that naive boundary narrowing harms grounding recall without improving overall localisation.

### 3.3 Gemini Post-Verification Toggle

In this setting, Gemini acts as a **candidate post-processor / verifier**: after VideoJev detects and refines candidate proposals, Gemini verifies each candidate clip, pruning proposals where `p_present < 0.5`. This is distinct from replacing the detection backend (evaluated in Section 2). The table below compares performance on identical proposals with verification toggled on versus off across the three composite benchmark videos.

| Dataset | True Events Retained | Recall (Off → On) | Complete Clip Rate (CCR) (Off → On) | Total Candidates (Off → On) | Absent-Prompt Candidates (Off → On) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BDD-X | 25/34 | 47.2% → 34.7% | 94.1% → 92.0% | 153 → 48 | 4 → 0 |
| YouCook2 a | 12/12 | 100% → 100% | 83.3% → 83.3% | 308 → 38 | 59 → 1 |
| YouCook2 b | 9/9 | 75.0% → 75.0% | 77.8% → 77.8% | 277 → 37 | 71 → 1 |

In cooking, total candidates dropped by 87.2% (from 585 to 75) while preserving all 21 previously detected true events. In driving, however, 9 true events were erroneously pruned, causing recall to drop by 12.5 percentage points. In YouCook2 a, 5 candidates encountered API failures; per the system's fail-open policy, these candidates were retained for human review rather than being treated as negative responses.

**Conclusions:** Automated post-verification exhibits stark domain divergence. While evidence supports its effectiveness in cutting reviewer workload in cooking, it does not justify enabling destructive pruning across all domains. Consequently, automated verification remains disabled in production. Because the 53 long videos were evaluated with verification turned off, the candidate reduction rates observed in this small sample cannot be extrapolated to the broader benchmark.

- Long videos: `python scripts/heldout_bench.py score OUT run`
- Composite benchmarks: `python scripts/composite_bench.py score DIR run` or `run_gem`
- Charades-STA: `python scripts/charades_sta.py score DATA_DIR RUNS`

The Gemini detection adapter script is located at `/root/vg/bench/gem_engine.py` on the test host; its checksum is recorded in the snapshot. It has not yet been merged into the repository as a production backend. The overarching operational conclusion is to retain VideoJev as the primary detector, maintain recall-oriented provisional settings, keep human review mandatory, and focus iterative development on driving false negatives, proposal boundary completeness, and reviewer burden reduction.
